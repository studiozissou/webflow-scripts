# Goal plan: how to get [client] to [goal]

> **When to use:** before a client's first goal-led report (`goal-report.md`). The plan sets the
> realistic goal, the phases, the goals the report tracks, and the tasks the report picks from.
> Write it as a Claude Doc for the client, then agree the goals with them. Until they agree, the
> report marks the goals "(proposed)".
>
> **Research inputs:** Semrush (rankings, volumes, difficulty, page-one results, backlinks,
> competitors), the latest site audit, Search Console if you have access, the client's Google
> profiles, and the live site. Date every figure.
>
> **Wording:** about 80% ASD-STE100, as in the report. Sentences of about 15 words, one word for
> one thing, terms defined once.

---

## The goal

One bold sentence with the realistic goal, then two to four sentences:

- What is realistic and what is not, and why (who holds the top spots, and how far ahead they are).
- Rough timings for each part of the goal, marked as estimates.
- The build as a count of tasks by size (for example "12 small, 8 medium, 3 large"), the recurring
  work once built, and any access that makes the work cheap.
- What must be agreed first, such as the monthly budget.

## Where [client] stands

- A table of the searches that matter: monthly volume, the client's position today, the page
  Google chose, and the 12-month target.
- What the results look like (local map, AI Overview, which kinds of sites hold page one).
- The gap that matters most, with a comparison table (for example authority score and linking
  sites against the sites on page one).

## How the pieces fit together

One short paragraph, or a diagram: which kind of search each page should win, and what sits under
all of them. Say where to start, and why.

## The plan in phases

Three to five phases, numbered from 0. Phase 0 is always **Measure**: tracking, starting figures,
lead source, data access. Each later phase has one plain name ("Win local searches", "One
national page", "Build authority") and a list of what it does. Say how changes are released (in
small batches, measured before the next) and what is rolled back if a batch loses ground.

## Goals for the monthly report

Every goal belongs to exactly one phase. The headline goal is the business result (leads,
bookings, sales), not a ranking. Rankings support it.

| # | Phase | Goal | Owner | Starting figure | 12-month target | Source | Automated? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | [headline business result] | Shared | [figure or "unknown until …"] | Higher than the same month last year | [system] | Yes, once … |

- **Owner** is "Will", the client, or "Shared". Goals the client owns are marked as theirs.
- **Source** names the system and how the report reads it. A goal with no source today says what
  unblocks it.
- Then a short "How the report changes" list: goals table after the Summary, same month last year
  comparisons, client goals marked, trends rather than single positions where data is noisy.

## The work, with sizes

| ID | Work | Phase | Goals | Blocks | Impact | Size | Client input | Automated? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | [task] | 0 | [goal #s] | [task IDs that wait on this] | high, medium or low | S, M, L or XL | [input, or –] | [Yes, Drafts or No] |

- IDs carry the phase letter (F = foundation, then one letter per phase).
- **blocks** lists the task IDs that cannot start until this one is done. The report picks next
  month's tasks from this list first, then by **impact**.
- **Size** is a T-shirt size for effort and difficulty, not a time estimate. Never convert it to
  time, in the plan or in the report.
  - **S**: one sitting, one person, no input from anyone else.
  - **M**: several steps, or one client input or approval.
  - **L**: several pages or systems, several inputs, or a release that must be measured.
  - **XL**: a project of its own. Split it into smaller tasks before it starts.
- A totals table by phase: the count of tasks of each size.

## What runs itself and what needs a person

A table: task · how often · automated? · who · size. Then one sentence on the recurring work for us
and for the client as a count by size, what an agency alternative would cost (marked as a typical range, not a quote), and
any rule that stays manual on purpose.

## What to expect and when

A table: when · expected result · how we measure it. Mark the timings as estimates, and say what
they assume (for example a number of new linking sites).

## Risks and how we manage them

A table: risk · what we do. Always include: rankings rise while the business result stays flat;
the data source under-reports; seasonal and update noise; the client's inputs stall; the goal
wording sets the wrong expectation.

## What we need from [client]

A numbered list. The first two unblock the most work. Name the person when one person owns it.

## Sources

Each source with its date: Semrush database and date, Search Console export, site audit, live site.
