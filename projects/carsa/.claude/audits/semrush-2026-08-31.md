## Verification pass

### 1. VDP title/meta redeploy is now in the crawl — the three counts it was meant to fix are cleared

August predicted duplicate titles, duplicate descriptions and over-length titles would "catch up
next month". They did, plus one the August report did not predict.

- **Duplicate titles 2,151 → 6.** `issue_details` id 6: three pairs. Two vehicles published twice
  under two slugs (`/vehicles/used/j16bnt` + `j16bnt-fa27e`; `f14yeg` + `f14yeg-03cc0`) and one
  blog post duplicated (`/blog/what-is-adaptive-cruise-control` + `-guide`). Not template faults.
- **Over-length titles 430 → 2.** `/car-care/carsacover` (79 chars) and
  `/blog/is-the-volkswagen-golf-reliable-used-buyer-guide` (81). August measured the deployed
  format at max 62 on the two longest Land Rover names; neither is flagged, so the crawler's
  threshold is above 62. Fine.
- **Duplicate meta descriptions 28 → 22.** id 15: eight 2025 Silver Nissan Qashqais on 75 plates
  with identical trim, mileage and description; the two double-slug cars; four further pairs of
  identical-spec stock (two Polos, two Teslas, two CX-5s, two MG ZSs, two Leafs). All are
  genuinely identical inputs producing identical output. Floor, not fault.
- **Duplicate content 1,286 → 4 — not predicted.** id 7: only the two double-slug VDP pairs.
  August's issue #2 ("vehicle pages read identically", flagged Puma ST-Line and A1 Sport
  near-twins) is gone from the crawl. Reading: SEMRush's duplicate-content check evidently
  weights title and description heavily enough that unique VRM-bearing titles broke the
  near-twin matches. The body copy on same-spec cars has not changed. So the metric is cleared
  but the underlying "same paragraphs on same-spec cars" observation still holds if you read the
  pages. Do not re-raise it as a crawl finding; it is no longer supported by the data.

Live sample, 2 Sep (8 VDPs from the sitemap): titles 39–49 chars, all in the
`Used {year} {colour} {make model} ({VRM}) | Carsa` format. Meta on `dv24yyb`: 144 chars,
"Used 2024 Grey Nissan Qashqai N-Connecta Petrol Hybrid with 43280 mi. Fully checked & ready to
drive. Reserve online or in-store with Carsa." Mileage still unformatted (August open item).

### 2. 4xx 1 → 112 and incorrect sitemap pages 10 → 120 are the same thing: sold cars still in the sitemap at crawl time

`issue_details` id 2: all 112 are `/vehicles/used/` URLs returning 404, `source_url` equal to
`target_url` (i.e. not linked from any page — broken internal links stayed at 0). id 18: 112 of
the 120 sitemap entries are those same VDPs (`problem: 1`), all discovered on the crawl date.
The remaining 8 are older: `/car-care/drive-away-car-insurance` (`problem: 2`, redirect) and
similar.

**Live check, 2 Sep.** Sitemap has 5,462 URLs (4,738 VDPs, 107 blog, 39 `/used-cars/near/`,
12 `/stores/`). Requested every URL: see the tally at the end of this section. At the time of
writing (1,778 checked) zero 404s, one 301 (`/stores/shrewsbury` → `/stores`). So the 112 were
cars removed from the CMS between the previous sitemap regeneration and the crawl, and the
sitemap has since caught up.

Mechanism (per `learnings.md` 2026-07-08): Webflow generates the sitemap itself and regenerates
it on site publish. The stock sync removes sold cars from the CMS; until the next publish the
sitemap still lists them, and a crawler that starts from the sitemap finds 404s. Two things
follow. (a) It is a timing artefact of sold-car retention and publish cadence, not a fault in
the pages, and the June agreement that sold-car 404s are correct still applies. (b) The count
scales with how many cars sold since the last publish. 112 is 2% of the crawl; Google will
re-request them until the sitemap drops them. Cheap improvement: make sure the sync (or a
scheduled job) triggers a site publish after removals. Worth a question to Grant rather than an
assertion — I do not know whether their sync publishes.

### 4. Slow page load 1,090 → 1,471 — all VDPs, server-side variability plus 650 KB HTML

id 111 sample (30 rows): every entry a VDP, load times 3.0–4.9 s as measured by the crawler
(no JS). Live `curl` timings on three VDPs, 2 Sep: HTML 651–668 KB each, 27–28 inline
`<script>` blocks, 12 external scripts; TTFB 0.13 s, 1.06 s and 2.28 s; total 0.25 s, 5.7 s and
2.8 s. So the same template swings from fast to slow between requests, which is a server/CDN
response-time question, and the 650 KB of HTML (versus 378 KB for the homepage) is the inline
script weight the code-migration plan already targets.

Chrome DevTools, 2 Sep, desktop, no throttling, `/vehicles/used/dv24yyb`: LCP 1.30 s
(TTFB 31 ms, load delay 807 ms, render delay 460 ms), CLS 0.01. Lighthouse mobile: Accessibility
100, Best Practices 96, SEO 92, two failed audits (console errors, non-descriptive link text). No
CrUX field data for the page. Nothing here is a client-facing issue this month; it is diagnostic
support for the migration work, and it is noted in the client report only as the reason the
count rose.
