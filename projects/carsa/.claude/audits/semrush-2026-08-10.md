## Verification pass

Every claim below was checked against something other than the crawl summary. Method noted
per item.

### 1. Structured-data errors are sold cars, not in-preparation cars — July's diagnosis is superseded

July reported 1,207 errors and attributed them to "cars still in preparation — they went live
before being photographed." That is not what the data shows in August.

**Method.** Pulled `issue_details` for issue 45 (25 pages), fetched each page's raw HTML,
parsed the JSON-LD, then rendered a sample in Chrome to see the post-JavaScript state, and ran
Google's Rich Results Test.

**Findings.**

- All 460 errors are `MERCHANT_LISTING` items missing the required `image` field, all under
  `/vehicles/used/`. No FAQ errors remain on make or model pages — that part of July's report
  holds.
- In raw HTML, every flagged page carries `"image": ""` — an empty string, not an absent key.
- 25 of 25 sampled flagged pages had `image: ""`. A control group of 10 currently-available
  cars all had a real image URL. The empty-image signal maps exactly onto sold stock.
- Rendered in Chrome, a flagged page (`yg21jdo-03b61`) shows a visible "Sorry! This car has
  been sold." banner, and the JSON-LD has `availability: SoldOut` with `image` and `price`
  both stripped. A control page (`ck23pxg`) renders with the banner hidden, `InStock`,
  `price: 9193`, and a real image URL. Confirmed on a second flagged page (`ye23ffk`).

**Caution.** The sold banner markup is present in every VDP's static HTML and hidden by
default, so it is not usable as a static discriminator. Only the rendered state or the empty
`image` field distinguishes sold from available.

**Interpretation — CORRECTED 10 Aug after checking meeting history.** My first read was that
Carsa had newly implemented the "keep sold pages live" approach we recommended in July, and
that this explained broken links going 56 → 0. That was wrong on both counts. Meeting record:

- **15 Sep 2025 planning call:** "For sold cars, show 'Sorry, this car has been sold'" — the
  sold state was specified nearly a year ago. It is not new.
- **10 Jun 2026 SEO call:** "404 VDP pages: 38 vehicle pages returning 404 — these are sold
  vehicles that have been removed from Webflow after a retention period; agreed this is the
  correct use of 404 and does not need fixing." So sold pages live for a retention window,
  then 404 by design, and the client has already signed that off.
- **9 Jul 2026 weekly sync:** "Dead links to sold cars: filtering issue on a carousel; likely
  resolved." That — not any sold-state change — is why broken internal links fell to zero.

Corroborating the "long-standing" reading: 10 of the 25 flagged pages have carried this error
since the 12 May crawl (`discovered` timestamps), which predates all of the July work.

The verified finding stands: the 460 errors are sold vehicles whose price and image are
stripped from the listing data during the retention window. Only the causal story was wrong.

Per the template's "meeting context overrides scan data" rule, sold-vehicle 404s must not be
reported as faults in future months.

**Google Rich Results Test**, `/vehicles/used/yg21jdo-03b61`, run 10 Aug 14:30:
4 items detected, some invalid. Product snippets — 1 invalid item. Merchant listings —
1 invalid item. Breadcrumbs and Organization valid. Critical issue on the Product item:
`Either "price" or "priceSpecification.price" should be specified`. Non-critical: missing
`priceCurrency`, `aggregateRating`, `review`.

Note that Google flags the missing **price** as critical while SEMRush flags the missing
**image** — SEMRush crawls without JavaScript and sees the static `image: ""`; Google renders
and sees price and image both gone. Both point at the same sold-state behaviour.

**Open question for Carsa.** Stripping image and price from sold cars may be deliberate (to
keep sold stock out of shopping feeds). Worth confirming intent before changing it.

### 2. Links with no anchor text: the fix landed

**Method.** Parsed every `<a>` on the homepage and two VDPs, checking for empty text with no
`aria-label` or `title`.

Homepage: 199 anchors, zero with no accessible name. aria-labels are in place on the icon and
social components ("Home", "WhatsApp us"). Vehicle card links get their accessible name from
the card image's `alt`, which is populated and correct at model level ("Citroen C3" on Citroen
C3 cards — checked the linked pages to confirm the cards really are that model, they are).

The 11 remaining are on two legal pages: `/terms/vehicle-purchase` (3 links to
`go.carsa.co.uk/cc`) and `/terms/cookie-policy` (8 links to external privacy pages).

### 3. Alt text — July's issue #7 was not supported by the crawl data

July listed "Add image alt text" as issue #7. SEMRush issue 110 (Missing ALT attributes) read
**0** in both the July and August snapshots, so that issue should not have been raised on
crawl evidence. Verified directly: vehicle card images carry alt text. Dropping it.

### 6. Broken external links are a crawler artefact, not a fault

Will annotated July's issue #9 in Notion as "non-issue — whatsapp rate limiting crawler". Confirmed:
`issue_details` for issue 12 returns `https://wa.me/442046322989` with HTTP **429** on every row
sampled. The link sits in the site header, so it appears once per page — 5,489 flagged against
5,536 pages crawled, near 1:1. WhatsApp rate-limits the crawler; real visitors are unaffected.

Do not raise this as an issue in future reports. It will recur every crawl.

### 7. VDP title + meta format redeployed 10 Aug (after the crawl)

Will deployed a new SEO template on the Vehicles collection at ~17:00 on 10 Aug, ten hours
after the 06:27 crawl. **The August numbers are all pre-fix** — do not read the 430 / 2,151 /
28 counts as reflecting it.

Deployed (read off the Webflow SEO settings panel):

- **Title:** `Used {Registration year} {Colour} {Make model name} ({VRM}) | Carsa`
  e.g. "Used 2023 Black Kia Sportage (RF23YWH) | Carsa" — 45 chars.
- **Meta:** `Explore this used {Registration year} {Colour} {Make model name} {Trim} {Fuel type} with {Mileage} mi. Fully checked & ready to drive. Reserve online or in-store with Carsa.`

Measured against real field values before deploy (1,080–3,420 combinations, 60-char limit):

| Title format | avg | max | over 60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old (`Used YR COL MAKE MODEL TRIM 5dr \| FUEL \| Carsa`) | 70.0 | 100 | 82% |
| First proposal (kept fuel) | 59.0 | 84 | 40% |
| With trim added back | 59.8 | 83 | 44% |
| **Deployed (no fuel, no doors, no trim, + VRM)** | **46.9** | **62** | **4%** |

Field costs, mean chars incl. separator: fuel 12.2 ("Petrol Plug-in Hybrid" = 22), colour 5.7,
VRM 8.0, trim 11.9 (range 2–20).

Trim was kept out of the title specifically because the VRM sits last — a long trim truncates
the plate out of the visible snippet, defeating the uniqueness fix. Trim moved to the meta
description instead.

**Open item on the meta description.** Measured across 9,126 real combinations: avg 158.4,
max 188, **60% exceed 155 chars**. Fixed boilerplate is ~108 chars, leaving only ~47 for the
car detail. Truncation falls on the trailing CTA ("Reserve online or in-store with Carsa"),
not the car detail, so severity is low — but the CTA is lost on most pages. Shortening the
boilerplate (drop "Explore this ", trim the closing sentence) would recover it. Raised with
Will; not yet actioned.

Also cosmetic: mileage renders unformatted ("46937 mi") with no thousands separator.

### 8. Movements explained

- **Slow page load 3,928 → 1,090.** Same crawl scope (5,629 vs 5,487 checks), so the drop is
  real rather than a sampling artefact. Remaining flagged pages are VDPs at 3.0–4.8s.
- **Pages not crawled 17 → 40.** All 40 are `/vehicles/used/` VDPs, and all 40 were first
  discovered on the August crawl date. New inventory added inside the crawl window. Same
  pattern as June's spike of 100 that fell back to 17. Not a defect.
- **Pages crawled 5,678 → 5,536.** Inventory turnover. Read alongside 4xx falling to 1 and
  broken internal links to 0, this is stock cycling, not pages disappearing.
- **4xx = 1.** `/vehicles/used/sa70oul`, a single sold car.
