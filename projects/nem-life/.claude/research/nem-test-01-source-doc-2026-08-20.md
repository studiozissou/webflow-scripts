# 1 — Strategic description

*Waarom reageer ik zo?* is the first NEM TEST self-test and the primary micro-commitment
on NEMLife.com. The lowest-threshold first experience of the NEM approach — no sign-up, no
payment, just an honest answer to a question she already asks herself.

**Theme.** Unexplained reactions in the moment. The user recognises that she responds to
situations in ways she doesn't understand or doesn't want — too fierce, too quiet, too
compliant, too absent. The test gives that reaction a face without judging her.

**Measurement object.** Which of the five NEM Methode protection mechanisms drives the user
most strongly: fear, self-rejection, false hope, false power or emotional numbing. **The
mechanism names stay under the hood** — the user never sees them in the test or the report.

# 2 — Principles and decisions

**Measurement model.** Five mechanisms — fear (angst), self-rejection (zelfafwijzing),
false hope (valse hoop), false power (valse macht), emotional numbing (emotionele
verdoving). Each measured via four questions across four dimensions: Thought, Behaviour,
Body & energy, Situational.

**Question structure.** 20 questions, 4 per mechanism, presented in mixed order so the user
cannot derive the model. Each mechanism has a physical energy question with its own
direction:

| Mechanism | Body direction |
|---|---|
| Fear | constricting, pulling back |
| Self-rejection | sinking, downward |
| False hope | driven, pushing forward |
| False power | tensing, pushing away |
| Emotional numbing | absent, flat |

"Body & energy" and "Situational" questions are deliberately placed later — the first
questions are accessible and immediately recognisable.

**Dutch language choices.** Always "reacties", never "patronen". *Reactie* operates at the
moment-level where the method works; *patroon* is cumulative and has become a coaching
cliché. Name, promise and CTA all speak about reacting. The word "destructief" is avoided —
we speak of reactions that hold someone back or keep them stuck.

# 4 — Scoring model

**Answer values.** never/nooit = 0, rarely/zelden = 1, sometimes/soms = 2,
regularly/regelmatig = 3, very often/heel vaak = 4.

**Summation.** Four questions per mechanism, summed independently → 0–16 each.

| Mechanism | Questions (1-based) |
|---|---|
| Fear | 5, 10, 15, 20 |
| Self-rejection | 1, 2, 7, 17 |
| False hope | 6, 11, 12, 16 |
| False power | 4, 9, 14, 19 |
| Emotional numbing | 3, 8, 13, 18 |

## ⚠️ Tiebreak order — the authoritative statement

> On an equal highest score, a fixed preference order decides which protection mechanism
> leads:
>
> **False power → Fear → Self-rejection → False hope → Emotional numbing**
>
> This single order also settles a tie between three or more mechanisms. The order is
> defined clinically by Christel and is fixed for this test.

**Our code disagreed with this until 2026-08-20** — it led with self-rejection and
emotional numbing (MECHANISM_MAP declaration order). Corrected; see
`nem-test-scoring.js:TIEBREAK_ORDER`. Note there are **three** different orders in play in
this project — this one, the sheet row order (fear → self-rejection → false-hope →
false-power → emotional-numbing) and MECHANISM_MAP declaration order. No two agree.

**Relative interpretation.** The outcome is relative: not "how serious is it" but "which
reaction drives you most". That reading only holds above a floor.

- A mechanism may only be named from **8 points out of 16** — an average of "soms" across
  its four questions. Below that the differences carry no meaning.
- The floor of 8 applies to **primary and secondary alike**. One number, one rule.
- The minimum is **not a severity measure**. Scores, thresholds and rankings stay under the
  hood; neither the conclusion text nor the report ever refers to the height of a score.
- Highest-scoring mechanism is **primary**, provided it scores ≥ 8. Second-highest is
  **secondary** if it scores ≥ 8 **and** lies within **3 points** of the primary. A gap of
  4+ names no secondary. **At most one secondary, ever.**
- If no mechanism reaches 8, or the profile is flat, none is named and a generic conclusion
  text applies.

Primary, secondary, tie resolution and the minimum-score check are all determined
**client-side** before report generation. Claude receives the resolved outcome and does not
recompute it.

## Worked examples

**Example 1 — Mila, female, 29.** SR 14, FR 10, EM 7, FH 6, FP 4. Spread 10 → not flat.
SR highest at 14; gap to fear (10) is 4 → no secondary. → primary self-rejection, `01F-SR`.

**Example 2 — Susan, female, 42.** FH 14, FP 11, SR 9, FR 7, EM 3. Spread 11 → not flat.
Gap FH→FP is 3, within 3 → dual. → `false-hope_false-power`, `01F-FH-FP`.

**Example 3 — John, male, 38.** FP 13, FH 13, SR 8, EM 5, FR 4. Spread 9 → not flat.
FP and FH tie at 13. **Tie resolved by the fixed order: false power precedes false hope**,
so FP leads. → `false-power_false-hope`, `01M-FP-FH`.

**Example 4 — Femke, female, 34.** SR 4, FR 3, EM 3, FH 2, FP 2. *"the spread between the
highest (4) and the lowest score (2) is 2 points — **within 2**, so nothing stands out."*
Flat **and low**. No mechanism named, test ends at screen 4, no opt-in, no report.
→ `flat-low`, `01F-LOW`, shown with a Tally contact link.

> A flat-low outcome is not a negative result. It has more than one reading — the user may
> not recognise herself in the questions, or may genuinely experience these reactions less
> often having already done substantial work. The test cannot distinguish the two, so the
> conclusion text stays neutral about the cause. The contact link is an open door, not a
> concern.

**Example 5 — Peter, male, 47.** FH 15, SR 15, FR 14, FP 14, EM 13. *"the spread ... is 2
points — **within 2**"*. Flat **and high**. → `flat-high`, `01M-HIGH`, with contact link.

> Unlike flat-low, this points in one direction: someone at the top of the scale across all
> five is under real pressure. A report is built around one leading mechanism and this
> profile has none, so a generic report would be the weakest output the system can produce,
> for the user most likely to need real support. A direct route to a person is better.

Conclusion texts and intro lines are both selected **client-side and hardcoded (not CMS)**.
They live in the Google Sheet `NEM_TEST_01_Default_texts`:

## 5.1 Conclusion screen texts

Tab **"Conclusion texts"**. Structure:

- 5 single-mechanism texts, per gender
- 20 dual-mechanism texts, per gender — one per directional pair, primary first
- 1 flat-low text, per gender
- 1 flat-high text, per gender
- **54 in total** (27 keys × 2 genders, per language)

**Key convention — the client-side lookup.** The `key` column holds the mechanism key
(`false-hope`), the directional pair (`false-hope_false-power`, leading first), or
`flat-low` / `flat-high`. **All combination keys are directional.** Key + gender is the
identifier used client-side and passed to the report engine as the resolved outcome.

**ID convention — internal reference only.** `[test-nr][gender]-[abbreviation(s)]`. Singles
and flats use one abbreviation (`01F-SR`); duals chain leading then following
(`01M-FP-FH`). Abbreviations: FR fear, SR self-rejection, FH false hope, FP false power,
EM emotional numbing, LOW flat-low, HIGH flat-high. **The ID is not used in the lookup and
is not passed to the report engine.**

## 5.2 Intro lines

Tab **"Intro lines"**. The intro line is **the teaser on the title page of the report**
(see §6.4). One short line that gives recognition without naming or explaining anything.

- **25 intro lines** — one for every key that produces a report: 5 single + 20 directional
  dual. **flat-low and flat-high have no intro line**; those routes end at screen 4 and
  never generate a report.
- Coupled to the same key as the conclusion texts, so one resolved outcome selects both.
- **Not differentiated by gender.** One line serves both — the only pre-written text that
  does not follow the gender split.
- **Selected by the report template, never generated by the engine.** It is passed to the
  engine as *context* so the report can be written in the same spirit, but it never appears
  in the engine's output (system prompt §1.2).

**Editorial rules**

1. **First person — always "ik", never "je".** It sits directly below the test title on the
   title page and has to read as the user's own inner voice. A "je"-formulation reads as a
   judgement from outside; "ik" is a mirror from within.
2. One short sentence. No subordinate clause, no second sentence.
3. For dual keys the **leading mechanism opens the sentence**, the following mechanism is
   the turn. This makes direction audible — `fear_false-power` and `false-power_fear` are
   two different lines, not a reordering of the same words.
4. Recognition only — no label, no mechanism name, no explanation of what follows.
5. No slogan, no brand line.

# 6 — Report generation

## 6.1 Profile fields

Three values collected on screen 3. They steer **text selection and tone**, never the score.

- **Gender:** Female, Male
- **Age category:** 18-30, 31-40, 41-50, 51-60, 60+
- **Relationship status:** Single without children · Single with children · With a partner
  without children · With a partner with children · Other

**Age buckets.** The report differentiates into two groups, and **Claude derives the bucket
itself** from the supplied category: "18 to 50 years" (18-30, 31-40, 41-50) and "50+ years"
(51-60, 60+).

**Use per field**

- **Gender** — selects the conclusion text on screen 4, and differentiates Thought,
  Behaviour, Situational and Present-day cost in the report. *The intro line is not
  differentiated by gender.*
- **Age bucket** — differentiates **Behaviour only**.
- **Relationship status** — a modifier. Woven in opportunistically where it aligns with the
  primary mechanism, without its own text variant or report section, and **without
  speculating about what the status implies**.

## 6.2 Payload to and from the report engine

**Input — the engine receives per user:**

1. first name
2. gender
3. age-category (raw, e.g. "31-40 year")
4. relationship status
5. language of the test run (NL or EN) — derived from the site version, not asked
6. the five mechanism scores (0–16)
7. **conclusion key** — the resolved outcome, matching the sheet's `key` column
8. **the conclusion text shown on screen 4** — matching text (NL)/(EN) for that key+gender
9. **the intro line for that key**

> The conclusion text and the intro line together set the register the report continues
> from: the report picks up where the conclusion text left off and is written in the spirit
> of the intro line. Both are supplied as **context only** — neither is quoted, paraphrased
> or referred to, and the intro line never appears in the output because the template
> already places it on the title page.

**⚠️ Items 7, 8 and 9 are not currently sent.** `Generate Report`'s payload contains no
`conclusionKey`, no `conclusionText` and no `introLine`. Filed as a gap 2026-08-20.

**Output.** Claude returns **content only** — no formatting, no headings, no markdown. A
single JSON object, five keys, fixed order: `opening`, `reaction`, `origin`, `cost`,
`closing`. Each a plain text string in the language of the run. Strict valid JSON: nothing
before or after the object, no code fences, no markdown inside values, all five keys always
present. The automation applies a fallback if malformed.

**Which layer-2 blocks feed which key**

| JSON key | Layer-2 blocks |
|---|---|
| opening | none — written from the first name and the shown conclusion text |
| reaction | Core + Manifestation: Thought, Behaviour, Body & energy, Situational |
| origin | Origin — shared source, written against the primary mechanism |
| cost | Present-day cost |
| closing | none — no differentiated content |

`opening` and `closing` frame the report and are written per user, not selected from a
variant. `reaction` carries five of the seven blocks, which is why it is by far the longest.

## 6.3 Differentiation matrix — 61 variants

| Block | Differentiates along | Variants |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | generic | 1 |
| Core | generic | 5 |
| Manifestation — Thought | female, male | 10 |
| Manifestation — Behaviour | female / male / 18-50 / 50+ | 20 |
| Manifestation — Body & energy | generic | 5 |
| Manifestation — Situational | female / male | 10 |
| Present-day cost | female / male | 10 |
| **Total** | | **61** |

Behaviour deliberately stacks two axes. "Generic" means the block does not vary by gender
or age bucket — **not** that the text is identical for everyone: Claude always writes it
against the user's primary mechanism. Origin in particular is tied to that mechanism and
never rendered as a general explanation of protection mechanisms.

## 6.4 Report template (client-side)

Claude returns content only; the template assembles the PDF around it. **Nothing in this
section is produced by the report engine.**

**Title page**

1. First name of the person who completed the test
2. Date of report generation
3. **The intro line belonging to the resolved key**
4. The fixed disclaimer

No slogan and no fixed brand line.

**Body.** The five keys in fixed order. The template applies all formatting, headings and
typography — the engine delivers plain prose only.

**Fixed disclaimer** — one sentence, identical in every report, never varied:

> Dit rapport is geen diagnose. Het is een spiegel op basis van jouw antwoorden - puur
> bedoeld voor inzicht en bewustwording.

Same sentence as the second disclaimer variant on screens 5 and 6. **⚠️ The English wording
for EN test runs is still to be added.**
